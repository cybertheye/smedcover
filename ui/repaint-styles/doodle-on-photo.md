Turn the attached photograph into a playful photo-and-doodle picture: the real subject, photographed, with a childlike black-line story drawn over it.

1. Keep the main subject photorealistic and exact: its silhouette, perspective, proportions, layers, colours, textures and irregularities. Do not replace, idealise, illustrate or redesign it. Remove only unrelated surroundings.
2. Place it on a pure white field (#FFFFFF — not cream, beige, grey, gradient or paper texture) with generous negative space and only a faint natural contact shadow. The subject is the largest element, about 55–70% of the useful area.
3. Choose one visually dominant part of the subject whose shape suggests a story, and draw across much of it a single high-coverage narrative in primitive black lines: shaky, uneven, slightly broken, blunt, as if drawn by a young child. Reuse the subject's real edges, folds, stacks, slices and details as parts of the drawing, and leave the photographed surface visible beneath the lines.
4. Add only a few supporting marks — steam lines, sparks, little stars, speed strokes, wobble marks, crumbs, droplets, impact curves — and at most one or two faceless micro-figures with clumsy stick limbs doing a simple job. Everything secondary stays sparse.
5. Colour comes almost entirely from the photo; the doodles are black. At most one tiny accent colour taken from the subject.

Avoid: polished illustration, cute mascots pasted on, detailed or complex faces, filled cartoon bodies, smooth vector lines, busy backgrounds, glossy advertising effects, extra objects.
