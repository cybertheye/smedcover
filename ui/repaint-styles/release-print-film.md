Grade the attached photograph with a 2383-inspired motion-picture release-print finish. This is a colour and tone finish only: keep every person, object, pose, framing, detail, sign and edge exactly where and as it is.

1. Tone: a confident, print-like contrast curve with dense, clean lower tones in which the darkest useful textures stay visible (no featureless black clipping); midtones with enough density to remove phone-HDR flatness without dulling faces; the brightest values compressed progressively into a smooth soft shoulder that keeps the shape of local highlights; a clear white point — never beige, grey or faded overall. Keep the subject's microcontrast; take only the brittle digital edge off specular highlights.
2. Colour: subtractive-looking density — saturated colours feel deeper and more material, never neon. Restrain high-saturation reds, oranges, greens and blues before they clip. Separate warm and cool by the scene's own light rather than a global teal-and-orange split: creamy, amber or gently golden highlights only where the light is warm; subtly cool, cyan, blue-grey or green-grey shadows only where the scene supports it. Skin stays natural and dimensional, free of green, orange or magenta. Keep meaningful colours of clothing, paint, vegetation, sky and practical lights.
3. Adapt to the scene: daylight — neutral whites, modestly cool shadows, smooth sky roll-off; golden hour and backlight — protected luminous highlights, amber light, deeper cool shade, no orange flooding; tungsten interiors — warm practicals but skin not uniformly orange; neon and mixed night light — each practical colour kept, extreme saturation controlled, deep but readable blacks; vehicles, products, architecture — lines, edges and finishes crisp.
4. Halation: a thin, warm red-orange inner fringe with a soft low-opacity falloff, only where a genuinely bright highlight meets a darker neighbour — sunlit edges, windows, chrome, wet reflections, bulbs, hard rim light — irregular and exposure-dependent, visible at normal viewing size but localised. A broader, softer, low-saturation diffusion round only the brightest highlight clusters. Nothing on matte midtones, faces without hard backlight, or shadow edges.
5. Fine, organic, non-uniform 35 mm grain at low opacity, varying with tone; crisp photographic detail beneath the film finish.

Avoid: red outlines round every object, an orange haze, lens-flare streaks, veiling glare, dreamy diffusion over faces, white bloom that erases detail, dust, scratches, sprocket holes, gate weave, a faded vintage look.
