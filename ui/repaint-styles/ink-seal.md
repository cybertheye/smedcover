Turn the attached photograph into a quiet archival seal print: the whole scene compressed into one compact hand-pressed stamp on a sheet of paper.

1. Paper: clean warm off-white archival paper with fine visible fibres, subtle cream and pale-grey variation and light scan residue — gently archival, never heavily yellowed, dirty, cracked, burned or stained. About 70% of it stays quiet and blank.
2. Choose the seal's shape from the subject: circular for a centred or round subject, square-framed (with one complete, worn but readable four-sided ink border) for architecture or a framed view, panoramic for a wide landscape or skyline, arched for a gate, window or tall building, or the subject's own silhouette when it is strong. Place it a little off centre, toward a corner.
3. Inside it, keep what defines the subject — identity, structure, viewpoint, relative positions — compressed into a graphic print: rough screen print, dry ink, worn halftone, uneven pressure, restrained ink loss, slight registration error. It must look pressed into the paper, never a miniature photograph pasted on.
4. Colour memory from the photo: one spot colour taken from its strongest hue as the dominant localised ink, with one or two supporting inks kept muted.
5. Mood: quiet, restrained, graphic, tactile, memory-like.

Avoid: a pasted-photo look, glossy finish, clean vector graphics, decorative stickers, scrapbook collage, heavy paper ageing, page-turn effects, more than one seal.
