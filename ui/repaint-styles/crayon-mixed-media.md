<!-- Its mixed-media drawing grammar; the lettering, desktop-pet and campaign layouts are left out. -->

Redraw the attached photograph as a loose, hand-made mixed-media drawing on white paper, assembled from unequal layers.

1. Structure: thin-to-medium black (or muted green) pen lines with a few heavier marker passages. Every structural stroke wanders along its length — shallow dents, flat spots, pressure jumps, dry breaks, overshoots, open joins, imperfect corners — and changes thickness within itself. The geometry itself is naive: unequal slopes, tilted rims, off-centre openings, slightly inconsistent perspective. Colour may miss the outline by a few millimetres.
2. Colour: start from the photo's one to three identifying colours, in low-to-medium saturation (tea green, yellow-green, cream, pale yellow, fruit red, restrained brown…). Lay it with dry media assigned by material: a few broad, blunt side-of-crayon sweeps with matte granular deposits and visible paper tooth; light coloured-pencil scumbling; occasional dry pastel. A pale translucent wash only for liquid, glass, clear plastic, light or atmosphere.
3. Collision: one or two small photographic fragments of the real subject, cut in, as accents — never the whole subject turned back into a photo.
4. People are faceless, seen from behind, or reduced to functional contours with simple bending limbs. Charm comes from awkward scale and a clear action, not from big eyes.
5. Black is structural, not dominant; white paper is an active material. Keep generous negative space: objects placed on paper, not a filled sheet.

Avoid: smooth equal-width vector outlines, sticker borders, Bézier curves with a wobble filter, ruler-straight lines, airbrushed gradients, glossy 3D, plastic shading, oil paint or gouache masses, wet watercolour blooms, dense uniform crayon over everything, chibi faces, excessive props.
