Turn the attached photograph into a Photo Relic: a restrained modern print made from the photo's memory, pressed into warm ivory paper — recognisable but compressed, artful but not decorative, quiet but not weak.

1. Derive everything from the photo's visible cues. Identity: keep the whole relationship that lets the subject be felt — roof stack plus base and path, tower plus skyline, doorway plus wall mass, body lean plus ground, shoreline plus horizon, window grid plus wall plane. Concept: compress it into one short feeling ("quiet order", "held dusk", "wet neon", "warm stone"). Edge: turn one horizon, wall, roof, street, railing or shadow edge into a mark that builds the main form. Scale: small people marks, base lines or paths so it does not float.
2. Build one clear primary relic shape with modern printmaking marks: flat deep-ink blocks, softened edges, negative-space cuts, short bars, stacked arcs, sparse trace lines, base lines, tiny dots, measured breaks. Architecture becomes stacked arcs or bands, blocks, voids and narrow cuts; a skyline stays a group — one dominant tower, varied bars, a horizon base, a few light dots; landscapes and water become calm residue lines with one anchor; people become small irregular vertical marks without faces or limbs.
3. Palette sampled from the photo and restrained: deep blue, ink black, muted teal, grey-green, stone grey, dusk charcoal, with at most one small warm signature accent (vermilion, gold, lantern or sunset orange) placed like a seal. The source light survives only through mark hierarchy or a very quiet field.
4. The relic takes roughly 35–55% of the picture's width, readable at a glance on a phone, surrounded by generous warm ivory or off-white paper.

Avoid: a full watercolour or literal illustration of the photo, vague haze too pale to read, only colour fields or swatches, generic Bauhaus geometry, neon gradients, heavy texture, grunge, torn paper, tape, stickers, collage, film sprockets.
