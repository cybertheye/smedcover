Rework the attached photograph into the front artwork of an independent-label vinyl record — a designed visual world, not just a pretty filter on the photo.

1. Read the photo as raw material: its colours, light, objects, textures, place, atmosphere and emotional tension. Ask: if this moment became a record, what visual world and one primary symbol would represent it?
2. Infer the sound from the mood and let it set the direction: ambient → blur, natural forms, minimal composition, large negative space; indie rock → film texture, candid photography, selective collage, imperfect print; punk → rough black-and-white reproduction and photocopy texture; electronic → geometry, synthetic colour, precise grids; jazz → expressive photography, rich paper and ink; folk → natural materials, hand-made marks, paper texture; dream pop → soft focus, bloom, hazy colour, layered translucent images.
3. Combine two to four coherent treatments, never all of them: colour reconstruction (a deliberate grade or palette replacement — cool low saturation for distance, warm contrast for nostalgia, fluorescent accents for tension, a controlled cast for an artificial reality); temporal texture (film grain, dust, light leaks, fading, photocopy noise, misregistration, only when the mood justifies it); optical displacement (soft focus, motion blur, long exposure, bloom, reflections, scanning artefacts); collage and layering (photo fragments, drawings, maps, scanned paper, transparent textures in one controlled hierarchy); deliberate imperfection (unusual cropping, unequal negative space, overexposure, uneven ink).
4. One dominant symbolic anchor — an object, an absence, a shape, a space or a recurring mark from the photo — that carries the feeling; it may be small, off centre, partly hidden or unexpectedly cropped. Meaningful negative space and a clear viewing path.

Avoid: a literal story summary, generic beauty, crowded collage, several competing metaphors, universal distressing, a photo of a physical record or sleeve.
