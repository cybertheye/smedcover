Reimagine the attached photograph as a poetic hand-drawn illustration on white paper — a fresh drawing, not a filtered or retouched photo.

1. Identify the main subject, its setting, and one or two memorable details. Keep them recognisable in simplified form; do not copy the photo literally.
2. Draw the scene small and concentrated: the illustrated subject or focal cluster takes about 10–16% of the page (never more than 18%), placed where it sits in the photo. Leave 80–88% of the page as blank white or very light paper.
3. Make it feel handmade: delicate pencil edges, visible brush marks, watercolour softness, a light paper texture.
4. Colour: vivid but controlled, and only inside that small illustrated area where it matters; it never spreads into the empty paper.
5. A subtle collage, stamp or clipping hint is allowed only if it stays tiny and belongs to the drawing.

Avoid: a full-bleed image, a photo-filter look, flat vector treatment, large collage blocks, a dense layout, overly realistic retouching.
