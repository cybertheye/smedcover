Turn the attached photograph into a surreal pop collage: the photo is still the photo, but its world has been swapped out — black-and-white reality colliding with a flat-colour dream, with only one thing that is not right.

1. Reality anchor: keep the most recognisable subject (a person, an object, a building, a landscape) clearly recognisable within three seconds, at its place and size, desaturated to black and white with its photographic texture. Nothing covers the core of a face.
2. Flat world: replace two or three regions that have natural boundaries in the photo — the sky, the ground, a water surface, a wall — with huge flat matte colour shapes following those boundaries: a disc (sun or moon), an arch, a horizon band, a whole sky. Derive the colours, never default to a red sun and blue sky: purify a colour already there (grass green → emerald, taxi yellow → lemon), collide with its complement (a warm orange scene → ultramarine), or invert the mood (a gloomy scene → bright yellow). Perfectly flat: no gradients, no shading, no 3D shadows on the colour shapes.
3. One impossible giant: exactly one, and it must come from the scene — something small already in the photo enlarged absurdly (a coffee cup bigger than the buildings), the thing most semantically distant from the place, a reversal of scale, or a visible translation of what the place is famous for. Never a stock whale or dolphin. A second impossible element is not allowed.
4. Small elements: one group of three to five small things from the scene (birds, leaves, paper boats…) in graduated sizes along one arc, and two or three white hand-drawn graffiti strokes (a brush mark, a wavy line, a star) as signs of the hand.
5. The whole picture stays bright and light: a dream, never dark or frightening.

Avoid: gradients, 3D shading on the colour shapes, imported unrelated objects, a frame round the whole picture, texture over everything, decoration over faces.
