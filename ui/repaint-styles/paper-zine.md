Reimagine the attached photograph as a poetic paper-texture, negative-space micro-editorial zine page: a flat scanned paper field carrying one small visual event.

1. Find the one element of the photo that carries its meaning — a figure, a fruit, a window, a stair, a cloud, a small slice of landscape — or a small relation between two things (two fragments nearly touching, an interrupted line, a window cut from a solid block, one repeated form with a part missing). That becomes the only visual cluster, about 8–25% of the picture, placed deliberately off the centre axis (centre-high, lower-left third, upper-right third…).
2. Give it one material form: a faded photo fragment, a clipping, a flat silhouette, a solid ink block, a small printed illustration, a translucent overlay, or an abstract texture window. Grey photographic parts stay subdued.
3. One clearly saturated accent hue — cobalt, ultramarine, cyan, violet, magenta-pink, lemon yellow, pear green, orange or tomato red — carried by the subject, a cut-out or a block, about 15–35% of the cluster and visible at thumbnail size. Do not weaken it to a pastel. At most one subordinate second hue.
4. The rest, 70–90% of the picture, is open paper: warm white, ivory, light grey, old-paper yellow, pale khaki or light kraft, with fibres, fine grain, faint dust, scan noise, slight ageing and matte absorbency. One or two quiet marks are allowed: a fine line, a dashed line, a small dot group, a registration mark, one hand-drawn curve.
5. Everything belongs to the same printed and scanned world: halftone, xerox softness, risograph grain, letterpress bleed or slight misregistration. Flat orthographic scan, diffuse light, low-to-medium contrast, no hard shadows.

Avoid: a full-bleed scene, a crowded scrapbook, multicolour chaos, glossy mockups, clean UI white, cinematic lighting, 3D, neon, cyberpunk, cartoon language, stock-photo realism.
