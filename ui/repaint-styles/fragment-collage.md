Turn the attached photograph into a witty editorial cut-out collage with one clear centre, the kind of picture a sharp social-media editor would make from life's fragments.

1. Keep what identifies the photo: a person's face, features, hairstyle, clothing, skin and pose; a pet's markings, face shape and expression; an object's key features. Do not swap faces, change age or gender, or replace a pet with a similar animal.
2. Cut the subject out and make it the centre — 45–70% of the frame for a person, or one core object or two main slices for a still life. Around it, use the photo's own material: enlarge one detail, repeat one small expression or object two or three times, crop a fragment into a strip. Add one to three small objects that belong to the same life, but no unrelated large objects and no random decoration filling every gap.
3. Choose one collage material and use only that: torn paper with photocopier grain; tape, sticky notes and hand-drawn arrows; newspaper clippings, tickets and scanned paper; or clean digital cut-outs with crisp white margins. Labels, tickets and notes stay blank — shapes only.
4. Colour: a three- or four-colour high-contrast system taken from the photo — black + yellow + white; blue + yellow + black + white; orange + acid yellow-green + black + white; red + cream + black; green + white + black with one bright accent; or for a portrait, colours from the clothes (cobalt + black + white + silver, forest green + cream + black + orange, wine + grey + cream).
5. The humour comes from scale and arrangement — something too big, something repeated, something placed where it should not be — not from cartoon faces.

Avoid: mixing several collage materials, multicolour chaos, glossy 3D, generic stickers, emoji, a different person or animal.
