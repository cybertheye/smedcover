Rebuild the attached photograph as a stylised photographic night scene of the same place: a concentrated artificial light burning inside broad industrial haze, urban light pollution colouring the sky and the distance. It is a photographic reconstruction, not a faithful retouch and not a new science-fiction location.

1. Keep two to four location-identity anchors (the skyline, a building, a bridge, a street layout, a harbour, a storefront) so the place is recognisable; simplify incidental clutter and rebuild the frame around the look.
2. Choose one complementary light relationship: sodium amber or warm yellow against deep teal-green (harbours, warm storefronts, calm water); signal red against cold cyan-blue (streets, intersections); warm white against petrol blue (mixed practical lighting, roads, architecture); or magenta-pink against polluted cool green (bars, wet glass, interiors). The relationship matters more than the exact hue; the pair owns nearly all the colour, with charcoal, black, grey and truthful material colour between them.
3. The ambient counter-field is broad (a readable core over roughly a quarter to a third of the frame), darker and softer: sky, haze and distance, and also physical carriers — unlit facades, foliage, paving outside the lamp pools, water, glass, metal — reaching into the lower half. The emitted family is concentrated and brighter, about a tenth of the frame: signs, lamps, storefronts, a few windows, with traceable spill, facade response or reflection elsewhere. Building windows stay sparse midtone rectangles with dark gaps, not glowing emitters.
4. The air is luminous, dirty and deep: a permeating particulate film, never clean transparent air, a pure-black sky or an opaque fog wall. The sky is darkest and coolest at the top, glowing toward the horizon and the light clusters; contrast and saturation fall off with distance; tight bloom near foreground lights, wider softer halos deeper in.
5. Choose a surface state for the scene: dry polluted haze; post-rain (no falling rain, broken residual water and shallow puddles carrying the reflections — preferred for paved outdoor scenes); active rain catching the light; existing water as the reflective bridge; or condensation on glass for interiors.
6. Deep darkness that keeps low-contrast structure in buildings, trees, roads and boats; ordinary materials and scale stay believable.

Avoid: a full-frame red/cyan filter, cool colour only in the sky, dense neon signage, invented technology, a hero character, painted concept-art texture, plastic 3D, synthetic HDR, over-sharpened game-scene finish, crushed blacks.
