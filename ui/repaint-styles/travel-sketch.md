Convert the attached photograph into a fast, loose observational travel sketch, as if drawn on location in ten to fifteen minutes in a personal sketchbook.

1. Observation budget: one visual anchor, two or three supporting masses, at most five identifying details. Delete 50–70% of the visible minor detail; suggest repetition with only a few windows, poles, tiles, leaves or ripples; stop describing a region once its role is readable. Keep identity through silhouette, viewpoint, overlap and landmarks, not exhaustive detail.
2. Line: dark charcoal-black fineliner, medium and slightly inconsistent weight, fewer line marks than colour marks. Each major edge is one relaxed wandering contour or at most two passes. Horizontals rise, fall, bow or break; verticals lean and wander; roof edges disagree, corners miss each other, with blunt turns, overshoots, open corners and abandoned starts. Reinforce the focal silhouette; let secondary edges fragment or disappear. Perspective is mildly compressed and naive; buildings and trees may lean; foreground forms crop naturally.
3. Colour as selective notation, never logical: colour only about 25–40% of the objects, and within a coloured object only 30–70% of its area; keep 45–60% of the paper uncoloured. A colour may cross several neighbouring objects or stop halfway through one. One focal colour cluster, one muted colour repeated in two or three separate clusters, charcoal clusters near structural junctions, one quiet grey-shifted hue connecting the background.
4. Palette: warm off-white sketchbook paper; three to five colour families loosely from the photo, shifted toward warm or cool grey, saturation lowered by a quarter to a half — dusty, subdued, slightly weathered — with a charcoal or warm grey structural neutral; any vivid accent under a tenth of the coloured area.
5. Marker behaviour: a dry, water-based fibre-tip felt marker on ordinary sketchbook paper. Every colour area is made of countable discrete strokes (about 3–12 per cluster) with blunt rounded ends, matte opacity, slight streakiness and dry skips, narrow paper gaps between strokes, darker only through a second visible pass. Short upright bands for foliage, facades and cliffs; slanted bands for roofs; broken lateral dashes for water and ground. Strokes may overshoot or stop short of the contours.
6. Stop before it feels finished: raw and observational, some shapes unresolved or open at the page edge.

Avoid: watercolour paper, wet edges, pooling, blooms, bleeds, glazes, soft blending, smooth fields, alcohol-marker rendering, ruler-straight lines, perfect rectangles, regular grids, evenly repeated windows, a polished picturesque or editorial-watercolour finish.
