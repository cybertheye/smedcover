Reinterpret the attached photograph as a contemporary ink-wash editorial image: a flat, front-facing sheet of real paper on which the photo's subject is absorbed into ink.

1. Preserve and absorb: keep the subject, pose, horizon or identity that matters, at a readable scale — an integrated image field of roughly 35–65% of the picture, placed where it best balances the space (upper left, lower right, true centre, left-centre… whatever the subject's shape asks for). Keep some photographic detail crisp near the focal point; let the edges and less important regions dissolve progressively into irregular ink blooms, diluted washes, dry-brush loss, broken contours and paper-coloured gaps. Never a uniform grey watercolour filter over the whole photo, never a small rectangle pasted on the page.
2. Simplify before reducing: clear away the busy perimeter, background, props and signage; keep the emotional evidence, not every object. Build around one focal subject, gesture or relation.
3. Choose one ink behaviour and make it physical: dry-brush fracture, wet wash bloom, diluted transparent layers, pooled pigment edges, rubbed transfer, soft photocopy grain, or a near-erased ghost mark. Paper fibres, absorption, deckled spread and tonal dilution must be visible.
4. Choose the paper for this photo, not a habitual beige: clear or cool white (airy, snowy, precise), warm rice or pale ivory (warmth, wood, dusk, intimacy), mist grey or blue-grey (rain, lakes, fog, urban quiet), muted celadon or mineral green (spring, plants, tea), pale indigo or moonlit blue (night, winter, solitude), dusty rose-grey (tenderness, flowers), light ochre or unbleached fibre (earth, autumn, craft); charcoal or deep indigo paper only for night or drama, with light mineral pigment. Low chroma, with pulp variation and faint stains — never a flat digital fill or gradient.
5. Match the ink to the paper: neutral black or graphite on warm paper, blue-black or smoky indigo on cool paper, warmer carbon on green or ochre paper. At most one restrained modern accent (yellow, cobalt, tomato red, indigo, mineral green, ochre) where it helps the subject.
6. Leave a large quiet field of paper; let the subject float, crop, dissolve or sit off centre. Mood: quiet, contemplative, tender, distant or lightly surreal, as the photo suggests.

Avoid: a full-page traditional landscape, oversized architecture, large seals or brush calligraphy, aged yellowed xuan paper unless the photo is historical, glossy 3D, cinematic lighting, neon, crowded collage, stock-photo polish.
