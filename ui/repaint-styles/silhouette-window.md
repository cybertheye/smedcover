Rebuild the attached photograph as a tactile silhouette collage built on figure–ground exchange: positive and negative space swap roles, and the viewer completes the missing subjects.

1. Keep the photograph itself: its people, their count, order, poses, raised arms, joined hands, held objects, clothing, and the place (lake, mountain, road, sea, snow, sunset, rocks), with its own grain, focus, exposure and colour cast. Do not redraw or change any person.
2. Choose the mask that fits the arrangement: one connected, loosely simplified envelope round a close couple or tight group (keeping the gaps, raised arms and held objects that show how many there are); separate silhouettes at true scale and position for people spread through the space, related by colour and a few thin crude paper bridges; or one jagged cut-paper chain for people standing in a line. Edges look cut by hand with scissors.
3. Lay a large flat sheet of coloured paper over most of the picture (about 40–60% of it) and cut the chosen silhouettes out of it as windows, so the photo shows through exactly where the people or main subject are. Elsewhere, lay a few solid paper shapes of the same silhouettes over the photo, so the same shapes appear once as a window and once as a block. Keep one calm, quiet region of about a sixth of the picture.
4. One palette recipe, sampled from the photo where possible: electric (hot magenta, sky blue, grass green, off-white); field diary (sage, moss green, dusty pink, cream); travel poster (vermilion, pale blue, warm landscape browns, ivory); or youth archive (faded olive, cream, forest green, charcoal). Strong enough contrast that the windows read at once.
5. Physical texture only on the paper: scanned uncoated paper, visible fibres, slightly uneven ink density, imperfect hand-cut edges, subtle registration drift, matte surface, faded consumer-film colour in the photo areas. A few small naive hand-drawn stars as punctuation, never overpowering the mask idea.

Avoid: a generic scrapbook, heavy noise over everything, fake torn-paper shadows, thick white sticker outlines, a sepia wash, redrawn faces or bodies.
