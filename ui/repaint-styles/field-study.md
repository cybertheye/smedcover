Translate the attached photograph into a minimal, restrained, mature editorial "field study" illustration — not a watercolour filter and not a full redrawing.

1. Find what makes the photo recognisable — the subject, its pose or orientation, outline, colour proportions and spatial relations — and delete every real detail that does not affect recognition. Keep the photo's memory point, not all its information.
2. Ground: a flat, clean warm cream (#F0EBDD) with no paper texture, grain, folds or frame. Never fill the canvas with the photo's sky, wall or surroundings; keep large quiet empty areas.
3. Composition: a visual specimen, not a full scene. The subject and supporting elements together take about 20–40% of the canvas (a small single subject 8–20%), usually off centre, keeping the photo's direction, hierarchy and centre of gravity; marks stop or fade before the edges.
4. Colour: two to five main colours from the photo, low-saturation, laid as flat watercolour-like shapes — one main colour per object, at most one small accent. No gradients, volumetric light, real shadows, gloss or modelled colour; only a little translucent edge.
5. Line: thin, restrained ink lines with slight hand instability, for outline, structure and key expression only — usually three to twelve key lines in all, perhaps one thin ground or horizon line. No hatching or repeated outlining.
6. By subject: a small isolated subject stays small, with one simplified shape and a few lines, perhaps one soft ring or block behind it; people and animals keep their most telling pose and expression in one to three colour shapes and a few lines, without hair, skin texture or folds; buildings, vehicles and ships become one continuous dark silhouette or a few geometric blocks with one confident contour (a few tiny coloured dots for lights at night); landscapes compress into two to four flat layers in the lower 25–40%, forests into one dark serrated band, mist into three to seven pale horizontal strokes; flocks, shoals and crowds become rhythmic small leaf, almond or dash shapes keeping the density, flow and colour proportions.

Avoid: a complete traditional watercolour, real materials (fur, fabric, cracks, leaves, metal reflections), photographic noise, cartoon, cute, picture-book or sticker style.
